## What I built

I systematised the screening into a repeatable workflow: reading tender documents, extracting requirements, applying explicit eligibility criteria, and tracking opportunities — with AI assisting the first-pass analysis and a person making the call.

## Architecture

The workflow is a chain of small, single-purpose AI skills rather than one large prompt. Each does one job and produces something a person can check:

- **Search.** A weekly scan of development-finance portals with a fixed set of queries, limited to the last seven days.
- **Title filter.** A cheap first pass that sorts dozens of opportunities by title alone and produces a download queue, so the expensive reading only happens where it is worth it.
- **ToR screening.** The full terms of reference are tested against the firm's hard disqualifiers, a *consultancy or implementation?* test and a *core share* test. The output is a label (IN, CONDITIONAL or REJECT), a short description and the main risks. The skill is told never to invent missing information: if the document doesn't say, the answer is "not specified".
- **Formatting.** The result is written straight into the 15-column tracker the commercial meeting already uses, so nobody has to learn a new tool.

**The feedback loop.** Every week, the board's real decisions and comments are added to a calibration log that the screening skills read before deciding. The system learns how the team actually reasons, not just what the written rules say.

The firm's own criteria and client data stay private. This page describes the design, not the contents.

## Results

The workflow reduced analysis time by approximately 30%, and feeds the weekly commercial meeting where I present bid/no-bid recommendations to management.
