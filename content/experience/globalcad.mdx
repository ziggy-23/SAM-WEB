## Overview

GlobalCAD is a Barcelona-based consultancy specialising in international development. I have worked across almost every stage of the consulting process: finding opportunities, writing proposals, doing the research, monitoring institutions — and improving how the organisation works internally.

## What I did

**Business acquisition.** I manage the screening of international tenders and funding opportunities from the European Union, UN agencies, development banks and public institutions — reading long terms of reference, assessing eligibility and technical expectations, and shortlisting the strongest opportunities for the weekly commercial meeting. Once we decide to bid, I help turn the call's requirements into structured technical and financial proposals.

**Research.** I contribute to Climate Risk and Vulnerability Assessments across regions and sectors — water, sanitation and hygiene, public health and education — working through datasets, existing research, focus groups and stakeholder interviews. I particularly enjoy bringing data to life: moving past tables to what the information actually says about the communities and systems being studied.

**Institutional work.** I track countries' commitments and progress for regional organisations, including a comparative climate-adaptation analysis across 20+ Mediterranean countries for the Union for the Mediterranean, and coordinate with counterparts such as UNICEF and Yemen's Ministry of Water and Environment. I have contributed to UNDP proposals on decarbonisation and innovation, and drafted organisational policies on safeguarding (PSEA), artificial intelligence and data protection.

**Technology.** I research and present AI tools in internal workshops, identify repetitive processes, and automate them where there is a clear gain — including the tender-screening workflow that cut analysis time by roughly 30%.

<Learned quote="Innovation does not necessarily require inventing something new. Sometimes it means looking at a process repeated hundreds of times and asking whether there is a better way to do it.">

GlobalCAD gave me a practical understanding of what international development looks like beyond academic discussions and policy documents. Development consulting is, in many ways, an exercise in connecting different worlds: donors and implementers, international priorities and local realities, technical expertise and political constraints.

I discovered how much happens before a project ever reaches the field. An intervention can begin as an international call for proposals, pass through pages of eligibility criteria, become a methodological and financial offer, and only then turn into years of research and implementation.

The research changed how I approach information. I became comfortable with imperfect datasets and contradictory accounts — and learned that interviews and focus groups are windows into experiences that can easily disappear behind aggregated figures.

Above all, it taught me to move between disciplines. In the same week I might analyse a climate vulnerability assessment, prepare a financial proposal, draft a safeguarding policy and experiment with an AI workflow. The most interesting problems rarely fit neatly within one field.
