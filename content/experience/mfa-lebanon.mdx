## Overview

My time at the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Emigrants was an early look at how public institutions work, and at the practical side of diplomacy.

## What I did

**International Organisations Department.** I supported documentation and administrative work — organising records and maintaining the institutional files behind the Ministry's engagement with multilateral bodies.

**Emigrants Department.** I worked closely with the Head of Department on a diaspora strategy to strengthen ties between Lebanon and its communities abroad: improving communication, developing initiatives and events, encouraging engagement with expatriate networks and building relationships with foreign embassies.

**A post-COVID study.** I contributed to research on the vaccination experience of Lebanese citizens abroad — how accessible and effective vaccination systems were in different countries — gathering information through interviews and focus groups and consolidating the findings into a broader report.

<Learned quote="It is often the less visible work that allows the more visible work to happen.">

The Ministry gave me a different understanding of diplomacy: one that begins long before anyone enters a negotiation room, and continues long after a meeting ends. Behind every official communication is a considerable amount of preparation, coordination and documentation.

The diaspora strategy was especially meaningful. Lebanon has one of the most dispersed communities in the world, and the relationship with its expatriates is central to its economic, social and cultural identity. It raised questions I still find fascinating — about belonging, identity, investment, and the role of a state in staying close to citizens who live thousands of kilometres away.

The vaccination study showed me the value of comparative research, and how interviews bring individual experiences into institutional analysis.

Above all, it made diplomacy feel personal. Countries are also people, families and communities whose relationships extend far beyond their borders.
