## Overview

My dual degree with the American University of Beirut makes me a graduate of Sciences Po as well as AUB. My concentration was in the ethics of war and governing climate change. The time I spent in Paris was a chance to experience one of Europe's most influential academic and political environments from the inside — in a city whose history is inseparable from modern political thought, diplomacy, revolutions and treaties.

International affairs were never treated as distant subjects. There were lectures, conferences and public discussions with ministers, candidates and academics — and sessions at the French National Assembly, where I watched debates on immigration, climate policy, pension reform, the subsidised price of bread, artificial intelligence and Europe's technological future.

My time there coincided with the outbreak of the war in Gaza in October 2023, which made following international developments from Paris particularly significant.

<Learned quote="International relations is not confined to foreign ministries and summits. It is equally present in debates about the price of bread.">

Reading about a parliamentary debate is one thing; sitting in the National Assembly and watching representatives argue over policies affecting millions is another. Being physically present made the link between academic knowledge and political practice feel immediate.

Paris also reminded me how intertwined the domestic and international really are — pensions, immigration, technology regulation and climate policy are all foreign policy too.

And there was Paris: a morning lecture, an afternoon political discussion, an evening discovering another neighbourhood. It made the study of international relations feel less like a discipline and more like a way of observing the world.

</Learned>
