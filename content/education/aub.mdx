## Overview

The American University of Beirut is one of the places that will always remain part of my personal and intellectual history — less a university than an institution woven into the story of Beirut and the wider Middle East.

Political Studies gave me the foundations: political systems, international relations, comparative politics and the institutions that shape societies. My minors in International Law and in Human Rights and Transitional Justice let me look at the same questions through legal, historical and ethical lenses.

Much of it happened outside the classroom: long days and nights on campus, research with professors, and more maps drawn and redrawn than I could possibly remember.

<Learned quote="Some institutions remain important because of what they teach you; others because of who you become while you are there. AUB is both.">

AUB taught me to approach political questions critically, to take institutions and legal frameworks seriously, and to never analyse the present without its history.

Research taught me something just as important: good work is rarely produced in a single attempt. An argument has to be tested; a map can always be more accurate. Those countless revisions were part of learning how to think carefully.

And beyond the academic experience, I associate AUB with Beirut itself — a campus overlooking the Mediterranean, in a city that has always felt like a meeting point between worlds. Whenever I can, I will go back.

</Learned>
